## 01 — Al Jazeera Arabic Live (`bNyUyrR0PHo`)

- **Cold open (frame_0s):** No cold open — it's a live news channel. Pure newsroom timbre with the studio room-tone bed.
- **Arabic linguistic register:** Pure, traditional MSA (فصحى) — court-grammar level. Anchor uses full case endings (إعراب) when reading the breaking strap: `غارةٌ إسرائيليةٌ على بلدةِ زوطر الغربية`. Verbs in the present-imperfect indicative with explicit subjects. Zero colloquial intrusion. The guest analyst on the right panel speaks slightly looser فصحى مبسطة but still avoids dialect markers. This is the "neutral pan-Arab MSA" that every Arab from Casablanca to Mosul understands and which carries authority but feels distant — distinctly NOT the Iraqi voice Photonect is building.
- **First hook line:** Visible on the red BREAKING strap throughout the open: **`عاجل · مراسل الجزيرة: غارة إسرائيلية على بلدة زوطر الغربية جنوبي لبنان`** ("Breaking · AJ correspondent: Israeli airstrike on the town of Zawtar al-Gharbiya in southern Lebanon"). The hook is the geographic precision (named village + cardinal qualifier).
- **Mid-video hook:** Around 35–60s the strap rotates: `الجيش الإسرائيلي: نستعد لاحتمال إطلاق نار من لبنان مستهدفًا منطقة الشمال` ("Israeli army: we are preparing for possible fire from Lebanon targeting the northern region"). AJ's mid-hook technique = swap the strap, never the chyron — viewer eye stays anchored to the bottom-left red rectangle while top-of-frame imagery rotates.
- **Cut cadence:** Almost zero cuts inside the 60s window observed. The four quadrants all move in their own time — field smoke pillar continues to plume, ruins are static establishing, hilltop is a slow live zoom, anchors do natural studio gesture. A live channel's "edit" is the lower-third change every 8–14s. Photonect lesson: the *strap rotation* IS the cut.
- **Visual treatment & Arabic kinetic type:** This is the *baseline* every Arabic viewer knows. Two-line strap, white type on AJ-red `#C8102E`-equivalent rectangle, RTL flowing right-to-left, line break is grammatical (verb-subject phrase ends line 1, prepositional phrase begins line 2). Letters are kashida-stretched (تطويل) for justification — note `إ‍س‍ر‍ا‍ئ‍ي‍ل‍ي‍ـ‍ة` and `ج‍ن‍و‍ب‍ـ‍ي` show the diacritic-less elongation that AJ uses to lock both lines to the same right edge. This is the *single typographic move* Photonect Arabic reels still don't deploy — proper kashida balancing across 2-line stacks. The AJ logo (calligraphic gold) sits on a white circle bottom-right; ticker below is a separate red bar with a different story scrolling RTL at ~60 px/s. Three layers of red, three different type weights, full hierarchy.
- **Audio architecture:** Anchor voice center, room-tone bed, no music, no SFX. Field reporter call-in audio is filtered (telephony lo-pass) so the viewer instantly knows it's remote. Audio register changes carry as much info as the visual ones.
- **Cultural register:** What lands for an Iraqi/Gulf/Levant viewer: (a) the village name "زوطر الغربية" — only a Lebanese viewer will know exactly where; AJ trusts you do. (b) the immediate Hezbollah subtext — never spoken, but every Levantine knows "south Lebanon strike" = the frame. (c) the analyst's red-tinted bookshelf backdrop (a quiet AJ visual code for "Lebanese-affairs guest"). None of this lands for an English viewer. The silent contract: AJ assumes you already know the geography, the actors, the war.
- **Story structure (60s window):** 0–8s anchor headline; 8–22s strap settles, viewer reads; 22–40s analyst Q&A intro; 40–60s strap rotates to IDF response. The narrative arc is *fact → context → opposing voice* in 60 seconds, repeated infinitely.
- **What it does that current Photonect Arabic reels don't:** Proper kashida-balanced two-line MSA strap; layered red banner / ticker / chyron hierarchy; allowing the field shot to *breathe* with no on-screen text for 2-3 seconds at a time so the VO-equivalent (the strap) carries the whole information weight. Photonect cards still over-pack every frame with 3+ text elements — AJ proves you can hold a viewer with one perfectly-set Arabic sentence and a smoking ridgeline.
