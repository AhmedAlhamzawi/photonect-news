## 01 — Best of Newsbroke (Farewell)
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3rTiYpH9UM4
Duration: 14:45 | Resolution: 640x360 | AJ+ comedy show finale (host: Francesca Fiorentini)

**Cold open / hook:** Host on camera, brick-wall set, talks-to-camera, mid-laugh. "Hi, I'm Francesca Fiorentini, and 91 weeks ago we started a comedy offshoot called …" — flubs the line. The flub IS the hook. No graphics intro, no music sting; the broken-take is the open. Lower-third "FRANCESCA FIORENTINI / @FRANIFIO" floats in by 2s with neon-pink/black aesthetic. Tiny "NB" channel bug, top-left.

**Anatomy of the sketch:**
- Set: a single brick-wall shot, purple/magenta moody key-light, host at chest-up framing. Zero B-roll for the first minute. ALL the energy comes from her face and writing.
- 91 episodes summarized as nicknames, analogies, and reaction GIFs. The piece is essentially a montage of insult-rhetoric: "the shamwow guy of presidents," "a caramel that fell on a barbershop floor," "America's shit-filled perogi." This IS the form — high-density rhetorical jabs strung together, no expository connective tissue.
- The closing is a Bone Thugs-N-Harmony cover ("And I'm gonna miss everybody when I'm gone"), then a sunset card "Thank you for watching. Newsbroke forever!" cursive font on orange sun.

**For NEWS reels — what's portable:**
- The brick-wall single-shot host setup. Zero set complexity. The "set" is a wall + a key light.
- Lower-third bug always visible (top-left "NB" + bottom name lockup) so any 5-second snippet is brand-attributable. Photonect could do same: top-left "NEWS" mark always on.
- Density of analogies. Where we say "sanctions cascade" they say "America's shit-filled perogi." Stronger visual language. Riskier register. The Iraqi-copywriter sub-agent could absolutely lean harder into surprising metaphor.
- The closing sunset card with cursive overscript = a usable loop-pattern. Photonect could end on a sun-disc + Tajawal-Bold call-to-action and it'd land.

**Don't copy:** the talking-head-only structure. Eight unbroken minutes of one face won't hold IG. We need the field B-roll cuts they earned by season 2.

## 06 — How Muslims Influenced Thomas Jefferson and America's Founders
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VLRCk68BZ00
Duration: 24:43 | Resolution: 854x480 | **PBS production** (NOT AJ+) — host Aymann Ismail in archival/library settings

**NOT AJ+** — PBS bug top-left throughout. But the FORMAT and host approach are exactly what AJ+'s Origins-of-everything line does, so the lesson holds.

**Cold open structure (this is a clinic):**
- 0s: ornate Library of Congress arches — wide painted-arch architectural shot with a single rim of light. Curious orchestral score. Bug = PBS, top-left.
- 2s: Cut UP — Corinthian columns from low angle, same room. Movement is a slow tilt. Camera is doing the work of the cut.
- 5s: Wider — coffered dome of the Library, statue silhouette in foreground frame-right. Three architecture frames in a row. NO host yet. Place is the protagonist.
- 12s: Host (Aymann) walks through marble-and-red-arch corridor toward camera. Beige collared shirt, neutral tones; dressed to disappear into the building. Walking-toward-camera shot.
- 30s: Reading-room interior — librarian fetching books at the back; foreground is two reading lamps with copper shades, leather-bound box. Composition is GENEROUS — host is smaller than the props. Books are the heroes.
- 30s VO: "I've come here to see two books that offer very different views of the early United States, and the place of Muslims within it." Two books = the dramatic question. Cold open ends on a problem statement, not a fact dump.
- End: PBS logo on black, fade. Slow fade-down rather than smash-cut. Earned silence.

**Voice / writing model:**
The host frames the entire 24-min doc around two physical objects (Jefferson's Qur'an + Omar Ibn Said's Arabic autobiography). Every interview — Library of Congress curators, Monticello historian, Mount Vernon archivist — is keyed back to those two artifacts. THE OBJECT IS THE OUTLINE. We tell stories from data; this tells story from object.

Notable rhetorical moves Iraqi-copywriter should steal:
- "These Muslims weren't imaginary future citizens from far away. They were real people — right here — struggling to keep their faith…" — direct challenge to the abstract framing the rest of the piece used. The doc CRITIQUES its own opening.
- "Fingerprints in the bricks… enslaved children, likely, are forming these bricks." Concrete moves your eye to a single physical detail. We tend to write at the level of "regime" or "deal." Move down to fingerprints.
- "The first iftar at the White House" (1805, Jefferson + Tunisian ambassador Mellimelli) is presented as a historical reveal, with stakes set up in the prior 10 minutes. We rarely sustain a reveal across more than 30 seconds.

**For NEWS reels — what's portable:**
- **Object-as-outline:** structure a 30s reel around ONE physical thing — a vote tally page, an ID card, a tank treadmark, a kerosene receipt. Build the entire post around making that object the protagonist. Way stickier than data dumps.
- **Place-before-host opens:** 5-12 seconds of architecture/landscape before any face. Iraq has plenty of architecturally rich news venues (parliament chamber, Kadhimiya gold dome, Basra refinery flares). Use them. Photonect does this once in a blue moon; should be standard.
- **Walking-toward-camera shot** (12s frame here) for the "in-the-field" beat. We don't have a host body, but we DO have a moving slow-zoom on a stills image — same emotional function. Push slow zooms further; current ones are timid.
- **Earned silence at the end** — slow fade to logo. Our reels currently end on a hard caption + music cut. A 1-second still after the last beat would feel premium.
- **Bilingual literacy moment** — when Aymann names "Surah Al-Mulk" out loud to the (white) curator, that's the doc's emotional center. For our (Iraqi) audience, the equivalent is naming a local detail — neighborhood, dialect, prayer time — that signals we're in-group. We do this; do it more.

**Don't copy:** 24 minutes of pacing won't survive IG. But a 60s "TWO BOOKS" reel built on this same frame would.

## Cluster takeaways for NEWS engine evolution

1. **Single-shot brick-wall talking head + dense rhetorical metaphors** (Newsbroke, video 01) is the most copy-resistant pattern. Their density of insult-analogies in 60 seconds is 3-4x ours. The iraqi-copywriter sub-agent should be pushed toward bolder figurative language — risk register shift.

2. **PiP debate / annotation layout** (video 02) is a valid format for any "two views on Iraq's election" reel. Floating thumbnail of the official statement upper-right, our analyst voice/avatar lower-left.

3. **Title-on-action** (video 03, 5s frame): the title card landing over a held human moment is more emotionally sticky than title-on-black-card. Test on next slate.

4. **FRONTLINE chapter cards** (videos 04/05, inferred): a single black-and-serif chapter title between scenes is a premium signal we don't currently use. Cheap to add.

5. **Geo-block reality** (videos 04, 05): the most directly relevant docs to our beat are inaccessible to our audience. That itself is a story. Also, watch-video.sh needs a `--proxy` option for cross-region retries — file as a separate fix.
