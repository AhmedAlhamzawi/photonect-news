**Cluster:** vox (Vox.com world-affairs explainers, channel: youtube.com/@Vox; plus one Johnny Harris piece — the former Vox Borders host).
**Sample:** 15 videos targeting Middle-East / North-Korea / Russia-Ukraine geopolitics — the same beat Photonect NEWS works in, but in English-language editorial-explainer form. The cluster spans the Vox house style (Sam Ellis / Joss Fong / Sam Posner / Sam Ellis direction), the original "Vox Explains" minute-pieces, the more recent map-driven "what's happening in X right now" cuts, and one post-Vox Johnny Harris essay (V11). Each video is 2.5–28 min long; we focus on the first ~30 s + the loop hook because that's the prosody Photonect's 30-60 s reels need to absorb. Frames were sampled at 0/2/5/12/30 s + endish so we could see the cold open, the first hook turn, the escalation card, and the loop. **14 of 15 videos analyzed; V02 skipped (YouTube served a Mr Terry "history teacher reacts" reaction video instead of the Vox original — see V02 block for details).**

## Top 5 cluster-level patterns (what Photonect should adopt)

### 1. Map-as-stage, with the typography mounted *inside* the world (V01, V04, V05, V14, V15)
Vox's signature move is a desaturated Mapbox satellite or hill-shaded GIS map that occupies the entire frame and houses the typography, country fills, route arrows, and date stamps within it. The map isn't a side-panel — it's the canvas. Numbers materialize as part of the geography ("4 failing states" types out *next to* the four highlighted countries; "September 6, 2022" advances *with* the salient). Photonect's reels currently use the map as a side card. We should make it the stage.

### 2. Cold-open with no narrator — let SOT, location chyron, or massive typography carry the first 3-6 seconds (V03, V05, V07, V09, V10, V13, V14)
Across the cluster the narrator does not enter for the first 3-6 s. The cold open is carried by (a) a politician's sound-bite (V03, V07, V14), (b) a found-footage explosion or strike (V06, V12), (c) a single satellite mystery shot with no chyron (V05), or (d) massive typography over propaganda b-roll (V09). The narrator delivers the *answer* after the audience has already asked the question. Our reels narrate from second one — we should learn to wait.

### 3. Source-credit chyrons as design system, not legal afterthought (every video)
Every Vox video keeps a small attribution chyron up nearly continuously: "North Korean Government", "CSIS Missile Defense Project", "France 24 | October 9, 2023", "Bellingcat", "Maxar", "Institute for the Study of War, Rochan Consulting | March 1, 2022", "@nexta_tv". The credit is integrated into the corner of the frame as a typeset element with a colored block. This is *credibility as design language*. Our reels generally just use the photo — we should adopt a recurring attribution chip in the same corner across every shot.

### 4. Music drop / diegetic verite for the loudest emotional beat (V03, V05, V06, V08, V12)
The biggest emotional moment in nearly every Vox piece is *not* the music swell — it's the music *cut*, with raw cellphone or news-helicopter or CCTV audio coming through naked underneath. V06's Beirut explosion + V03's Gaza siege quote + V12's Yemen marketplace CCTV all dump the score for 4-8 s. Our reels currently run music at constant level throughout. A planned score-cut is a powerful tool we don't use.

### 5. Foreign-language source preservation + Vox-styled subtitle bar (V13, V14)
When Vox uses Arabic-network footage (V13) or a Russian-language Putin SOT (V14), they preserve the original chyron and the original audio, and overlay a *Vox-styled* subtitle bar (chunky black banner with bold yellow caps text on V14, sans-serif lower-third on V13). The foreign-language chrome stays on screen; the Vox subtitle bar adds the translation. This is more authentic than dubbing or cropping — it tells the viewer "we are letting you see and hear the original source." Photonect publishes in Arabic for an Iraqi audience, but the principle inverts cleanly: keep the original-language audio of foreign politicians/networks/UGC, add an Arabic Vox-styled subtitle bar in our brand lockup.

### Bonus pattern (#6, repeated enough to mention): inverted-pyramid news-doc structure
Across V03/V04/V05/V06/V07/V12/V13/V14/V15, the cold-open is the *most recent* event (the strike, the explosion, the SOT, the seizure), then the narrator pivots to "but to understand this, we have to go back to…" The lede is now, the body is the history. This is the news-doc form, and it's what makes a 5-10 minute explainer feel relevant. Our reels often open with context, not consequence — we should invert.
