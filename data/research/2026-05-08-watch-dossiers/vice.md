# Vice News — Watch Dossier (2026-05-08)

8 videos in cluster. 7 watched (video 03 age-gated, near-duplicate of 02 noted at the end).

Cluster signature: **vérité over voiceover, silence as a weapon, branded chyron lockup.** Vice almost never uses score under fact. The Vice News logo (top-right) and burned-in caption blocks are the consistent visual chrome — every other surface is given to the world itself.

## 01 — The Islamic State (Full Length) | 42:31 | 480p, no transcript

- **Cold open (0s):** Black, almost-textured frame — pavement at night, no graphics, no music. Vice News bug top-right. The viewer is *already inside something* before they're told what.
- **First hook (2s):** Hard cut to inside-of-car POV, a fixer points out the windshield, burned-in caption "The sniper is based there." No narrator. No score. Letter of warning, in someone else's voice.
- **Mid hook (5s–12s):** Driving plate (oncoming truck through windshield), then stark cut to the iconic ISIS-flag-on-truck-bed pan with another caption: "We Muslims are the ones who want to enforce Sharia in this land." Caption is the only translation; ambient diesel/wind is the score.
- **Cut cadence:** Slow. Long takes — easily 8–15 seconds before a cut in the cold open. Once inside ISIS-held territory the camera drifts handheld instead of cutting. Silence between sentences is left in.
- **Visual treatment:** 16:9, no overlays besides logo + caption. Caption typography is white sans, soft drop-shadow, bottom-third. No name supers, no lower-thirds. Color is sun-baked, mid-contrast, no grade fx.
- **Audio architecture:** Diegetic only. No music bed in cold open or first 5+ minutes. Voices in Arabic, captioned. Wind, vehicle, footsteps. The absence of score is the signature.
- **Story structure:** Embed-as-thriller. No Q&A. The correspondent (Medyan Dairieh) is invisible — only the fixer and the militants speak. Ends on the Vice News production card on black — a deliberate refusal to "wrap up" with narration.
- **What it does we don't:** *Trusts silence.* Our reels stack copy + score + transitions on every beat. Vice's nerve here is leaving 6 seconds of road and one captioned sentence carry the entire opening. Worth stealing: the "fixer-as-narrator" device — let an Iraqi voice point at the threat instead of writing it.

## 02 — Charlottesville: Race and Terror | 22:05 | 480p, has transcript

- **Cold open (0s, 2s):** Black frame. Logo only (Vice News | HBO). Hold for ~3 seconds before any image. Audio leads — distant chants bleed in.
- **First hook (5s):** "HBO Original Programming" silver-grain card. Single sting. No music bed.
- **Mid hook (12s):** Tilt-shifted dim portrait of correspondent Elle Reeve in low key — establishing the *interrogator* before we see what she's interrogating.
- **Escalation (30s):** Hard cut to torch march, in-focus center subject, burned-in caption "Jews will not replace us!" Faces lit by torchlight, not by lighting kit. Caption sits in the lower-third.
- **Cut cadence:** Editorial restraint. Shots last 4–7s in the cold open, then accelerate to 1.5–3s during the march sequence, then *slow down again* for sit-down interviews (Christopher Cantwell stretches into 20s+ takes — letting subjects damn themselves).
- **Visual treatment:** Cinematic 16:9, shallow DOF interviews, real-light vérité in the field. Logo lockup top-right always. Captions white, sentence case, slight shadow. No B-roll graphics.
- **Audio architecture:** Almost no music. Tension comes from chants, ambient room tone, a single low drone under transitions. Subjects' voices carry — Cantwell's interview is dry-cut.
- **Story structure:** Three acts in 22 min — torch march night → daytime rally and the car attack → in-room with Cantwell after the fact. Reeve as on-camera correspondent, but more *listener* than presenter; she lets a Nazi monologue without interruption and the audience condemns him instead.
- **What it does we don't:** *Holds black.* Three seconds of nothing is forbidden in our edits but it's how Vice signals "this is documentary, not feed." Worth stealing: end-card subscribe screen is animated radial linework — clean motion that doesn't compete with content.

## 04 — Tobaccoland & Underground Heroin Clinic | 29:36 | 360p, has transcript

- **Cold open (0s):** Pure black, several seconds. Only the *teaser*-voiceover by Shane Smith starts — "this week on Vice…"
- **First hook (2s, 5s):** Two-up of HBO grain logos (HBO + HBO Entertainment). Restraint built into the bumpers.
- **Mid hook (12s):** Cigarette tray macro — visceral, sticky, brown-and-cream. Texture is the hook, not graphics.
- **Escalation (30s):** Cut from Indonesia to Mexico — the two-host two-shot at the seaside, talent + correspondent, sets up the *braid*: "I went here, Thomas went there."
- **Cut cadence:** Within segments, languid 6–10s observational takes; between segments, snap-cuts driven by Shane's anchor address. Two-segment 25-min hour: 12-min regulatory exposé + 12-min human-cost piece, mortared by host on-camera links.
- **Visual treatment:** Grade is cool-blue indoors, warm-orange outdoors. Stencil-cut "VICE" logo bumpers in the open and between segments. Lower-thirds for names and locations only — no info graphics.
- **Audio architecture:** Sparse beds. A single low pulse under Shane's anchor block, dry-cut for field tape. Music drops out the moment a subject speaks.
- **Story structure:** Anthology — a tonal pivot from absurd-but-light (smoking-cures-cancer clinic) to grim (heroin in Mexico). The format teaches you to sit through hardship by easing you in.
- **What it does we don't:** *Anthology pacing.* A NEWS slate has 6 stories that all live at the same temperature. Vice slows you down with story #1 so #2 lands harder. Worth stealing: pair a *light* MENA culture beat with a *dark* one in the same reel and let the contrast carry weight.

## 05 — Love in the Time of Fentanyl | 75:25 | 480p, has transcript

- **Cold open (0s, 2s):** Black, then black. The film *refuses* to start. Audio is the fade-up: an overdose-response radio call ("blood oxygen 25") leads picture by 4–6 seconds.
- **First hook (5s):** Macro of a gloved hand cutting open a Narcan kit. The *object* is the protagonist before any face.
- **Mid hook (12s):** Wide-portrait of a man preparing a syringe by a window, baseball cap pulled low. Sympathy by composition (window light, not harsh fluorescents).
- **Escalation (30s):** The overdose response — two outreach workers spraying disinfectant on someone slumped on the sidewalk. Camera is hip-height, not lying down — dignified, not voyeuristic.
- **Cut cadence:** Long. Average shot length 5–9s. Dialogue runs continuous, picture floats. The film *trusts faces* — a 20-second hold on someone's eyes is normal.
- **Visual treatment:** Theatrical aspect (2.39 letterboxed inside 16:9 in the YouTube cut). Cinema-grade interior lighting, color graded warm, *no* tabloid flash. End card is the ITVS logo — broadcast indie funder, not Vice — but the grammar is Vice-doc.
- **Audio architecture:** Ambient radio chatter, sometimes piano under transitions, dry-cut for first-person speech. No score during overdose scenes — only sirens and breath.
- **Story structure:** Subject-first. The overdose at minute 0 plants the *stakes*; the next 70 minutes are character work. No narrator. No talking-head expert. Subjects narrate themselves.
- **What it does we don't:** *Picture lags audio.* Vice fades up audio first, then images. We always cut picture-and-audio together. Try: bring in voice 1.5s before image — your viewer commits before they see. Worth stealing: refuse the talking-head expert; let a participant explain the world.

## Cluster takeaways for Photonect NEWS

1. **Hold black longer.** Every Vice piece opens 2–4s of darkness with audio leading. Our reels detonate with copy at frame 1; we're skipping the breath.
2. **Captions, not lower-thirds.** Vice burns in *speech* in white sans bottom-locked. We over-design with cards and tickers — kill 70% of chrome and let an Iraqi voice carry a sentence.
3. **Audio leads picture.** Across 5/8 cold opens, sound enters before image. Try fading VO in 1.5s ahead of cuts.
4. **Silence is a beat.** The ISIS embed and Charlottesville have stretches with *no music*. Our music-rotation rule says "never silent" — Vice argues the opposite. Carve out one silent beat per reel.
5. **Local voices, not narrator.** Fixer-as-narrator (01), eyewitness-as-narrator (08), creator-with-guide (06). Our reels narrate *at* the audience. Insert one Arabic-language captioned witness clip per slate.
6. **Then-and-now (07).** Recyclable format: 2003 archive + 2026 present + studio host. We have 6 weeks of our own archive — start mining it.
