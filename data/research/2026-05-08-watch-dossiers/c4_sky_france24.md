## 01 — Sky News Investigates: Inside Syria's Islamic State Prisons (Alex Crawford)
**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uEKo_JjyFTg • 9:25 • 854x480

**Cold open (0–2s):** No graphic, no studio. We're already inside Panorama prison. Alex Crawford (mask on, on-camera) walks down a sterile blue corridor flanked by masked guards in royal-blue jumpsuits. Door labeled "18". The whole frame whispers institutional dread — locked down, masked, fluorescent. Zero text overlay. The location IS the hook.
**First hook (2–5s):** Quick-cut to a tight close-up of an unidentified detainee, beardless, eye contact straight into lens. Cuts the sympathy-confusion gap fast: *who is this man?*
**Mid-hook (12s):** Combat B-roll — a fighter in fatigues firing a mounted .50 cal across palm-fringed countryside. Drops the geographic context and signals "this isn't just a feature, the war is live."
**Cut cadence:** Slow for a Sky piece — 3–5s holds on Crawford's walk-and-talk, longer on prisoner interviews (8–12s). The structural rhythm is institutional space → individual face → institutional space again. Almost no whip-cut energy; the unsettling quiet IS the texture.
**Visual treatment:** Natural fluorescent prison white-blue. Cinematic 24fps feel. No on-screen kicker fonts, no animated maps, no graphic chevrons. Lower-thirds appear only for named prisoners and only briefly. The aesthetic is "you're walking through a place no one walks through."
**Audio architecture:** Ambient prison reverb (footsteps, distant doors). No score under interviews. A single low drone bed comes in only over the CCTV-escape segment. Crawford's voiceover is intimate, conversational — never declarative.
**Story structure:** Hook (the cell door, masked guards) → personal protagonist (Hamza the British detainee, smiling on cue) → second protagonist (Swedish prisoner, evasive on Sharia) → systemic threat (CCTV of escapes, Molotov drills) → human cost (Al-Hol mother of five) → unresolved future. Classic feature arc, but built around access — the *fact of being inside* is the whole asset.
**What it does that we don't:** Sells access as the headline. We bury our killer location card in a stat layer; Sky News opens *with* the corridor and trusts the viewer to lean in. The audio confidence — letting silence + ambient reverb carry tension instead of a synth bed — is something our reels never try.

# Synthesis — what to steal for our reels

1. **Two-tier lower-third (France 24).** Top kicker (today's novelty) + bottom main (running story) — lets us carry continuity across the 6 daily reels without re-explaining context. Apply to BottomTicker.
2. **Permanent location pin (France 24, "PARIS" top-left).** A small, unmoving city/source label so the viewer always knows where the dispatch is from. We have nothing equivalent.
3. **Chapter-card design (Sky Yazidi long-form).** Black-textured wall + white text fade-in to break monotony every 8–10s. Adapt for HEKAYA's slow-tale format especially.
4. **Sustained single-voice holds (DW, France 24).** Let one voice — reporter or expert — carry 20–30s without cutaway. We over-cut. A still face IS dynamic if the voice is.
5. **Bilingual captioning (NY Post / aggregator).** Arabic + English subtitles when source footage is non-Arabic. We've been one-language even when the source is English.
6. **Pre-war / sequel structure (Channel 4 Mariupol).** When we have material on a person/place from a previous post, build the new reel as a sequel. Memorial card design ("name 1967–2022" on photograph) is the simplest tearjerker we never use.
7. **Attribution discipline (TOI aggregator).** Every B-roll clip stamped with source watermark. Even our authentic stock looks more journalistic when visibly cited.
8. **Editorial-question headlines (France 24, Sky).** "WHAT'S FRANCE'S ROLE?" and "WILL U.S. RESPONSE BE EFFECTIVE?" put the audience inside discourse. Our headlines mostly assert ("Sweida Erupts"). Try one question-frame per slate.
9. **The chyron-as-show (Sky News breaking bulletin).** Five simultaneous information streams (brand, section, headline, ticker, location/time) with clear hierarchy. Our footer is a single line — there's headroom.
10. **Audio confidence (Sky / Channel 4).** Letting silence + ambient reverb carry tension instead of a synth bed. Most of our reels have music throughout. Earning a *score-free* moment in the middle would be a real evolution.
