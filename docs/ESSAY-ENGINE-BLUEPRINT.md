# Audio Production for an Arabic Rapped/Narrated Economic Video-Essay — Research Brief

## TL;DR — Recommended Stack

**Two production modes, share the same alignment + bed layer:**

| Layer | Narrated mode (default, daily) | Rapped mode (hero/flagship pieces) |
|---|---|---|
| **(A) Vocal** | **ElevenLabs Eleven v3** TTS, MSA voice, with-timestamps endpoint | **Suno v5.5** full rapped Arabic track (Custom Mode, Arabic script lyrics) |
| **(B) Bed** | **ElevenLabs Eleven Music** instrumental (generated to spec, builds to outro sting) — OR Suno instrumental stem | Suno already produces the beat; isolate via stem download, or layer a separate Eleven Music bed under spoken intro/outro |
| **Timing** | ElevenLabs timestamps come free with the TTS call | **ElevenLabs Forced Alignment API** (feed it the Suno track + the lyric transcript → word + char timestamps) |

The single most important architectural finding: **ElevenLabs has a standalone Forced Alignment endpoint** that aligns *any* audio file to a transcript — so a Suno rap track (which has no timing metadata) gets the exact same word-level caption + EQ-bar timing data as your TTS narration. One alignment format feeds both modes. This is what makes "rap" and "narration" interchangeable in your Remotion pipeline.

---

## (A) The Vocal

### Option 1 — ElevenLabs (narration; NOT rap)
- **Quality:** Premium tier for naturalness. Eleven v3 is the most expressive TTS model, with audio tags ([excited], [whispers], pacing control) that make narration "a performance rather than a flat reading." Strong fit for an economic video-essay VO spine.
- **Dialect control:** Supports MSA, Egyptian, Levantine (Syrian/Lebanese/Palestinian), Gulf, Maghrebi accents. **MSA is its strongest Arabic mode**; colloquial dialects work "decently when scripts are simple" but it "does not differentiate by dialect as thoroughly as alternatives." For Iraqi specifically, expect to drive it via a Gulf/Levantine-leaning voice + carefully spelled script; there is no first-class Iraqi dialect.
- **Rap?** **No.** v3 and all its audio-tag documentation is narration/dialogue only — no [sing]/[rap]/rhythmic tags, explicitly positioned for "audiobooks, interactive characters, dialogue-driven media," not music. Do not expect a rap cadence from ElevenLabs.
- **Cost:** Creator $22/mo (121k credits ≈ ~120 min speech), Pro $99/mo (600k credits). TTS = ~1 credit/char on v3 multilingual (0.5–1 on Flash/Turbo). A ~60s reel script is trivially cheap.
- **API:** Mature. `POST /v1/text-to-speech/:voice_id/with-timestamps` returns audio + **character-level** timing in one call; a `calculate_word_times()` helper rolls chars up to words. Eleven v3 (Alignment) returns per-char AND per-word timestamps in seconds.

### Option 2 — Suno v5.5 (the rap)
- **Quality:** v5 vocals are "the most natural in any AI music model," 44.1kHz studio-grade mixes, songs up to 8 min. This is your rap engine.
- **Dialect control:** Use **actual Arabic script** (not romanized) in Custom Mode for correct pronunciation; specify regional tradition in the style prompt (Egyptian vs Gulf vs Maghrebi singing differ). Iraqi rap cadence is achievable by prompting style + writing the lyric in Iraqi-spelled Arabic, but it's prompt-craft, not a guaranteed dialect toggle.
- **Cost / rights:** Pro **$10/mo** (2,500 credits, ~500 songs) or Premier $30/mo — **both grant full commercial rights** (Spotify/Apple/social/distribution OK). Cheap.
- **API caveat:** **Suno has NO official public API** — generation is web-app + limited beta partners only. Third-party API resellers exist ($0.014–$0.111/song, or ~$19/mo subs) but are unofficial and a supply-chain/ToS risk. For a daily automated pipeline this is the main friction: either (a) accept a manual/semi-manual Suno step for hero rap pieces, or (b) route through a third-party reseller and accept the risk.

### Option 3 — Human VO

## Motif 1 — Audio-reactive EQ / waveform bars over b-roll

**Approach (fully native, no external lib).**
- Load audio with `useWindowedAudioData({ src, frame, fps, windowInSeconds: 30 })` — windowed, not `useAudioData()`, so a 58s reel doesn't load the whole buffer into memory on every Lambda frame.
- **EQ bars:** `visualizeAudio({ fps, frame, audioData, numberOfSamples: 256, optimizeFor: "speed", dataOffsetInSeconds })` → array of 0–1 values, low index = bass, high index = treble. Map each to a bar `height`. `numberOfSamples` must be a power of 2.
- **Oscilloscope/waveform line:** `visualizeAudioWaveform()` + `createSmoothSvgPath()` → one smooth `<path>`. Use this for the narration voice; use `visualizeAudio` (FFT) for the music bed.
- **Compositing over b-roll:** put `<OffthreadVideo>` (the AI clip) in an `<AbsoluteFill>`, the bars in an `<AbsoluteFill>` above it with `mixBlendMode: "screen"` or `"overlay"` so the EQ glows against the footage rather than sitting in a black box.
- **Critical gotcha for your codebase:** raw FFT is bass-dominated and ugly. Apply the log/dB scaling already documented in your local rule (`audio-visualization.md` → "Postprocessing"). And when the bars live inside a `<Sequence>` with an offset, **pass `frame` down from the parent as a prop** — do not call `useCurrentFrame()` inside the bar child, or the spectrum jumps discontinuously at sequence boundaries. This exact footgun is called out in your skill file.

**Snippet shape (drop into a `<NewsReel>` scene):**
```tsx
const { audioData, dataOffsetInSeconds } = useWindowedAudioData({ src: staticFile(audioSrc), frame, fps, windowInSeconds: 30 });
if (!audioData) return null;
const freqs = visualizeAudio({ fps, frame, audioData, numberOfSamples: 256, optimizeFor: "speed", dataOffsetInSeconds });
// render freqs.map(...) as <div> bars or SVG rects, mixBlendMode: "screen"
```

**Where CapCut/AE is easier:** CapCut has a one-click "Audio Spectrum" sticker; AE has the built-in **Audio Spectrum / Audio Waveform** generators (Layer → keyframe audio amplitude). Both are point-and-shoot. Remotion wins on *parametric* control (exact colors, RTL mirroring, brand glow, per-scene gating) and on reproducibility across 6 reels/day.

## Motif 2 — Animated big-number counters

**Approach (native, no lib).** This is the easiest motif and fits your existing `bigStat`/`supportingStats` props perfectly.
- Drive the value with `interpolate(frame, [start, end], [0, target], { extrapolateRight: "clamp", easing: Easing.out(Easing.cubic) })`, or a `spring()` for a slight overshoot "snap."
- **Formatting:** wrap in `Intl.NumberFormat` for thousands separators / currency / percent. For Arabic reels use the `ar-IQ` (or `ar`) locale so digits and separators render correctly: `new Intl.NumberFormat("ar", {...}).format(value)`. This is a real advantage over CapCut, which counts in Latin digits only.
- **Anti-jitter:** as digits change width the number "wobbles." Fix with `fontVariantNumeric: "tabular-nums"` (or a monospaced numeral font) and `font-feature-settings`. For Tajawal/Cairo (your brand fonts) verify tabular figures exist; if not, render each digit in a fixed-width `inline-block` cell.
- **Glow/punch:** scale the container with a second `spring()` keyed to the same start frame, plus a `text-shadow` glow that ramps with `interpolate`.
- **Make it audio-aware (your edge over CapCut):** add the `bassIntensity` value from Motif 1 to the scale so the number *throbs on the beat* while it counts — impossible to do cleanly in CapCut.

**Where CapCut/AE is easier:** neither has a true native counter — CapCut needs a plugin/template; AE needs the **Slider Control + expression** (`Math.round(effect("Slider")("Slider"))`) or a script. Remotion's `interpolate` is strictly simpler and locale-correct here. **Remotion clearly wins.**

## Motif 3 — Cinematic dissolve / morph transitions between AI clips

**Approach.** Use `<TransitionSeries>` (already installed) and pick the right preset, then go custom for "morph."
- **Built-in cinematic presets** in 4.0.448: `dissolve()`, `dreamyZoom()`, `filmBurn()`, `zoomBlur()`, `linearBlur()`, `iris()`, `clockWipe()`, plus `fade/slide/wipe/flip`. `dissolve()` is the literal cross-dissolve; `dreamyZoom`/`filmBurn` give the "AI morph" feel cheaply.
- **Timing:** `springTiming({ config: { damping: 200 }, durationInFrames: 25 })` for organic, or `linearTiming` for predictable. Remember every transition **shortens total duration** by its frame length — your `calculateMetadata` duration math must subtract each transition (your `transitions.md` rule has the exact formula; this matters because your render hard-fails on duration mismatches).
- **True luminance/displacement morph (the premium look):** write a **custom presentation**. The shape (confirmed against 4.0.448 docs) is:
  ```tsx
  type TransitionPresentation<T> = { component: React.FC<{children, presentationDirection: "entering"|"exiting", presentationProgress: number, passedProps: T}>, props: T };
  ```
  Inside the component, animate an SVG `<filter>` (`feDisplacementMap` driven by `feTurbulence`, or `feGaussianBlur`/`feColorMatrix` luminance threshold) where the filter's `scale`/threshold interpolates on `presentationProgress` (0→1). Apply it differently for `"entering"` vs `"exiting"` children. This reproduces CapCut's "liquid melt"/displacement transitions natively and reusably — and per your **Architectural Fix Mandate**, building it once as a presentation benefits every future reel.

**Where CapCut/AE is easier:** CapCut's transition browser (Morph, Liquid, Glitch, Zoom) is one tap; AE has Trapcode/native morph cut and far cheaper GPU displacement. SVG `feDisplacementMap` in Remotion is CPU-rendered and can be slow at 1080×1350@2x — budget render time, or pre-render the displacement noise as a static asset. **CapCut wins on speed-to-cool; Remotion wins on consistency + zero per-clip manual work across a 6-reel daily slate.**

## Motif 4 — Beat-synced cutting

**Approach (offline beat map → frame numbers; this is the one with no native API).**
- Remotion has **no beat-detection API**. Detect beats **once, offline**, in a build step and bake the result into props — never at render time.
- Run `web-audio-beat-detector` (`analyze()` / `guess()` → BPM + first-beat offset in seconds) on the **music bed** in a Node prebuild script, or for irregular onsets use `essentia.js` (BeatTrackerMultiFeature) for a full beat-time array. Output `beats: number[]` (seconds).
- Convert to frames: `beatFrames = beats.map(t => Math.round(t * fps))`. Feed these as the `durationInFrames` boundaries of each `<TransitionSeries.Sequence>` so **cuts land exactly on beats**. For a "flash on beat," add a `spring()` flash whose start frames are the `beatFrames`.
- **Two clocks, kept separate:** *cuts and flashes* sync to the **music** beat map (Motif 4); *captions* sync to the **narration** word timings (Motif 5). Don't conflate them.
- Determinism is preserved because the beat array is precomputed JSON — the render just reads numbers.

**Where CapCut/AE is dramatically easier:** CapCut's **"Beat" auto-marker** (tap or auto) and Premiere's beat markers are instant and visual. Doing this in Remotion is the most labor-heavy motif. **Recommendation:** keep beat detection as a small `prebuild` node script feeding your existing `props.json`; treat the beat array like any other generated input (same way you generate voiceover). Accept that this is where CapCut has the biggest ergonomic lead.

## 1. FORMAT DEFINITION

**Name:** `Essay` (channel-facing: "تحليل Photonect" / Photonect Deep-Take)
**Length:** 90–150s target; **120s** default hero length (vs. MISTER MONEY's ~108s). Variable, not fixed — this is the core structural break from NewsReel.
**Frame:** 1080×1920 9:16 vertical, **true 2.7K-class clarity** (render at 1080×1920, source b-roll at native 9:16 from the video model — no center-crop). Cinematic 2.39:1 *active band* with branded letterbox bars (the teardown correctly killed "2.39:1" as a MISTER-MONEY feature — we *add* it deliberately as an overlay, bars carrying logo + ticker, never dead black).
**FPS:** 30 (engine standard).

### The 7-beat steelman→critique→reframe→resolve arc
This is the format's spine — a *fair-debate* structure that earns trust by arguing the other side first. Each beat maps to N segments in the schema (`segments: z.array(Segment).min(4).max(12)`), so beat length flexes with the topic.

| # | Beat | Function | Runtime | Visual register | Tone |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook / Bait** | Pose the false binary: "best decision or worst?" | 8–12s | Cold open, headline glyph, EQ neutral (cyan) | Provocative, question-led |
| 2 | **Steelman** | Argue the *strong* version of the position honestly | 18–25s | Warm grade, building counters, EQ cyan/teal | Generous, "yes, and…" |
| 3 | **The Turn** | Pivot — "but…" — first crack in the case | 8–12s | Grade cools, EQ shifts red | Knife-in |
| 4 | **Critique** | The hard counter-case, **numbers over adjectives** | 25–35s | Red EQ, sourced counters, contrast cuts | Rigorous, sourced |
| 5 | **Steelman-back / Concession** | Grant the one fair point on the other side | 10–15s | Brief warm beat, single counter | Honest, disarming |
| 6 | **Reframe** | "But the *real* question is…" — relocate the debate | 18–25s | New visual motif, neon arrow, EQ amber | Clarifying |
| 7 | **Resolve + CTA** | The creator's calibrated verdict (the hedge), then Photonect outro | 12–18s | Brand card, follow CTA | Earned, calm |

**Tone:** First-person, conversational, MSA-default narration (rap reserved for hero pieces — see §2). Editorial-mandate compliant: every claim attributed, numbers > adjectives, no loaded language. The arc's *steelman* and *concession* beats are what make us more credible than the original, which only fake-debated.

**Iraq/MENA topic fit** (the format is built for high-stakes, contested, number-heavy national decisions — exactly our editorial lane):
- Iraq's $17B Development Road / Grand Faw Port — "نهضة أم دَيْن؟"

- Mega-projects: Baghdad metro, Common Seawater Supply, electricity import bill
- MENA-wide: Saudi NEOM/giga-project ROI, UAE sovereign AI spend, OPEC+ quota math
Each is a "we spent tens of billions — was it genius or folly?" essay with **real, sourced counters** instead of the original's unverifiable `824,061,456,290`.

## B. AI-video failure modes — where the clone looks WORSE, not better

The original @chebemoney looks "low-res contact-sheet" precisely **because slow dissolves over near-static cells hide every AI-video failure mode.** That ugliness is camouflage. Your upgrades strip the camouflage off:

- **Beat-synced HARD CUTS are an own-goal at this fidelity.** Hard cuts on the beat are exactly what expose temporal flicker, warping, and character-inconsistency — the eye lands on a fresh frame and instantly reads the seam. The original's "only ~3 hard cuts, rest dissolves" isn't a weakness you're beating; it's a *defensive choice*. At 24 clips × 5s with cuts landing on bass hits, you will showcase every morph artifact the model produced. Your "upgrade" amplifies the format's worst-looking moments.

- **Veo 3.1 Fast at ~$0.15/5s is the *cheap* tier — and the cheap tier is where faces melt.** Any clip with a human face (your "hopeful patient," "young researcher," "designers' hands gesturing," "masons finishing stonework" — all real prompts in your current slate) will show hand/finger artifacts and inter-clip face drift. The spec's negatives (`no text, no logos, no UI`) do **nothing** for anatomical morphing. You have no mitigation listed.

- **Character inconsistency across 24 clips is structural, not fixable with prompts.** A 7-beat essay implies a through-line. But each Veo clip is an independent generation — the "Iraqi worker," the "official," the "engineer" will be a different person every cut. The original sidesteps this with abstract money/city b-roll and no recurring human subject. If your essays feature any recurring character, the clone reads as incoherent in a way the original never does.

- **Arabic-text hallucination in-frame is your highest-probability brand-killer.** Veo *will* render fake Arabic signage, fake ticker text, fake UI — and unlike Latin gibberish, **garbled Arabic is instantly, viscerally wrong to your entire audience.** Your existing memory already has three guards for this (`KIE No-Screenshot Guard`, `Fake-UI on Gov Buildings`, `Persian-Char Guard`) — and those were written for *stills*, which you Read-verify one frame at a time. A 5s video clip has 150 frames; hallucinated Arabic can flash for 8 frames mid-clip and pass a cover-frame Read-verify. **Your QA is frame-sampled; the failure is temporal.** This is the gap most likely to ship a humiliating reel.

## C. The real bottleneck the spec under-weights: Arabic script + rap rhythm

The spec treats rap as a "hero-piece" garnish. It's actually the make-or-break quality axis, and it's the one you have least control over:

- **Suno's Arabic prosody is weak, and its rap-in-Arabic is weaker.** It mispronounces, stresses wrong syllables, and slips toward a generic MENA-pop melisma instead of bars. MISTER MONEY works in **Egyptian colloquial** — which raps naturally and which Suno has heard a lot of. Your mandate is **MSA-default** (memory: Editorial Mandate, copywriter mandate). **MSA does not rap.** It has no street cadence; forcing rap rhythm onto formal Arabic sounds either stilted or absurd. The spec's "rap reserved for hero pieces" hides that the hero format's core hook may be *structurally incompatible with your editorial register.* This is the deepest risk and the spec gives it one table row.

- **Forced alignment on rapped Arabic is the weakest link in your caption clock.** ElevenLabs Forced Alignment / WhisperX word-timestamping degrades hard on fast, melodic, dialectal Arabic with music underneath. When alignment drifts, your "glow-on-active-word" captions desync — and desynced RTL captions look *broken*, not stylish. The original's captions are crude but always in sync because they're hand-timed in CapCut. Your automated pipeline can produce a *worse* caption experience than the manual original.

- **You're inheriting two register problems at once:** the copywriter mandate forces MSA + attribution + numbers-over-adjectives (a *newsroom* voice), while the format demands first-person opinionated swagger (a *creator* voice). These fight. Run your essay script through `iraqi-copywriter` and it'll sand off exactly the provocative edges that make the format work. Pass it through unsanded and you've broken your own mandate. The spec's "steelman beats make us more credible" papers over the fact that **the voice that makes essays land is the voice your mandate forbids.**

## D. Cost/time creep — the daily-cadence math doesn't close

The spec's "~$4–5/video, ~30–45 min" is best-case, single-take, zero-reroll fantasy.

- **AI video never one-shots.** Realistic reroll rate for usable cinematic clips is 1.5–3× (faces, hallucinated text, wrong motion). 24 clips at 2× rerolls = **~$7–11/video in Veo alone**, not $3.60. At a daily narrated slot that's ~$200–330/mo of b-roll, not "well under $50–100."
- **Render time is understated.** 1080×1920 @ 30fps × ~3600 frames with `<Video>` compositing, per-beat LUT, grain, vignette, EQ bars sampling windowed audio, and 24 crossfading clips is **not** 15–25 min locally — video decode + per-frame audio analysis is far heavier than your current stills-based NewsReel. Lambda fixes wall-clock but adds per-render cost and a whole infra dependency the spec mentions in passing.
- **Read-verify of 24 clips × 150 frames is not "5–10 min."** Done properly (temporal Arabic-hallucination check), it's the dominant human-gated cost and it does not parallelize. The spec's own QA section admits luminance is sampled at "N fractions" — sampling cannot catch an 8-frame hallucination flash.
- **The honest per-video wall-clock for a quality-passing essay is hours, not 30–45 min.** Which is why Phase 3's "optionally add a daily narrated essay as a 7th daily slot" is the most dangerous sentence in the doc. It risks the live 6×/day revenue path's compute, attention, and QA budget for a format that isn't proven once.

## E. Brand / credibility — opinionated essays vs. your signal-only mandate

This is the strategic landmine, and the spec actively rationalizes past it.

- Your entire channel identity (Editorial Mandate, in memory) is **signal-only, attribute-every-claim, no loaded language, numbers-over-adjectives.** The Essay format is, by definition, **a calibrated opinion** — "genius or folly?", "the *real* question is…", "the creator's verdict." You cannot have a "first-person economic argument" and "no loaded language / signal-only" simultaneously. The "steelman→critique→reframe" arc is a *rhetorical* structure; it persuades, which is the opposite of signal-reporting.
- **Iraq/MENA mega-project essays are politically loaded by default.** "Development Road / Faw Port — نهضة أم دَيْن؟" is a direct value judgment on a flagship government project. An AI channel posting daily opinions on Iraqi state economic policy invites accusations of agenda, foreign-influence, or factional alignment — the kind of attention a faceless automated account cannot defend in comments. The original gets away with it as an Egyptian personality; you'd be an **anonymous bot editorializing on Iraqi sovereign spending.** That's a different, worse risk class.
- **Audience confusion / feed dilution.** @photonect.news trains followers to expect neutral 53s news. Dropping a 120s opinionated rap-essay into that feed reads as off-brand and can depress reach on *both* formats. The spec's "never touches the live path" is true at the *code* level and false at the *brand* level — they share one audience and one handle.

## The 5 things you MUST nail for the prototype to land

1. **Arabic rap/VO quality — settle it FIRST, before any code.** Generate 3–4 Suno Arabic rap takes (MSA *and* Iraqi colloquial) of one real script and listen. If MSA can't rap and colloquial breaks your editorial mandate, the hero format is dead and you pivot to *narrated* essays only. This is the gate. Don't build the engine until this is answered.
2. **A temporal Arabic-hallucination QA gate, not a cover-frame check.** You need an automated pass that samples every clip at high frame-rate (or an OCR-on-Arabic detector) and *rejects any clip with in-frame Arabic/Latin text or fake UI.* Without this, you will ship garbled-Arabic reels. This guard must exist before clip #1 goes to render.
3. **Pick the cut style that hides AI artifacts, not the one that flaunts them.** Lead with motion-matched dissolves and slow pushes; reserve hard cuts for the 2–3 abstract/text-free moments. Beat-sync the *grade/EQ pulse*, not the clip boundary. This single choice decides whether the clone looks premium or melts on screen.
4. **One real end-to-end hero video, manually QA'd by Ahmed, before any cadence talk.** No "7th daily slot," no schedule. One Iraq essay, full arc, real Veo b-roll, real rap, real captions — proven to clear your own QA checklist with zero errors caught by Ahmed first. Kill Phase 3 from the plan entirely until Phase 2 ships once.
5. **Resolve the voice/mandate contradiction explicitly.** Decide and write down: is the Essay track signal-only (then it's analysis, not opinion, and you tone down "genius or folly?"), or is it an opinion track (then it lives under a *visibly distinct sub-brand*, not blended into @photonect.news's neutral news feed)? Half-measures here damage the core channel.
